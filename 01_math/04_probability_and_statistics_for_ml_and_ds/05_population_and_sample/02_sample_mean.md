# Sample Mean

Suppose you want to estimate the average height of the world’s population. Measuring everyone is impossible, so you take a small sample, calculate the average height in your sample, and use that as an estimate for the population mean. This approach works for other statistics, like proportions, but behaves differently for variance.

Let’s revisit Statistopia, where the population size is 10 and everyone’s height is known.

- **Population mean ($\mu$):**  
  Calculated by averaging all 10 heights.  
  Example: $\mu = 160$ cm.

If you can’t record all heights, you randomly select a sample of 6 people.

- **Sample mean ($\bar{X}_1$):**  
  Average height of the first sample of 6 people.  
  Example: $\bar{X}_1 = 160.97$ cm.

Take another random sample of 6 people:

- **Sample mean ($\bar{X}_2$):**  
  Average height of the 6 shortest people.  
  Example: $\bar{X}_2 = 156$ cm.

Comparing $\bar{X}_1$ and $\bar{X}_2$:
- Both use 6 people, but $\bar{X}_1$ is a better estimate because it was randomly sampled.
- $\bar{X}_2$ is biased.

Now, try a smaller sample size ($n=2$):

- **Sample mean ($\bar{X}_3$):**  
  Average height of two randomly selected people.  
  Example: $\bar{X}_3 = 158$ cm.

**Key Point:**  
The larger your sample size, the better your estimate of the population mean. Random sampling is crucial for accuracy.
