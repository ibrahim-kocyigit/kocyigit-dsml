# History of AI

- AI has experienced several cycles of high excitement and investment, followed by periods of disappointment and reduced funding ("AI Winters").
- The field began with early algorithms and optimism in the 1950s, but faced setbacks when initial expectations were not met.

## Key Historical Milestones

### 1950s: The Birth of AI

- **1950:** Alan Turing develops the **Turing Test** to evaluate a machine's ability to exhibit intelligent behavior indistinguishable from a human.
- **1956:** The term **Artificial Intelligence** is coined at the Dartmouth Conference, marking the official start of AI as a field.
- **1957:** Frank Rosenblatt invents the **perceptron algorithm**, an early model that could learn from data—a precursor to modern neural networks.
- **1959:** Arthur Samuel publishes a checkers program that learns from experience, popularizing the term **machine learning**.

## The First AI Winter (1960s–1970s)

- **1966:** A US government committee assesses machine translation (especially Russian-English) and finds little progress, leading to reduced funding.
- **1969:** Marvin Minsky identifies major limitations in the perceptron algorithm, further dampening enthusiasm.
- **1973:** James Lighthill's report highlights the gap between AI's promises and actual progress, prompting further funding cuts.
- These setbacks led to the **first AI Winter**—a period of reduced investment and interest in AI research.

## The Second Boom: Expert Systems (1980s)

- **Expert Systems:** Rule-based programs designed to mimic human experts became popular in business applications.
- Ran on mainframe computers using languages like **LISP**.
- AI gained large-scale business adoption for the first time.
- **Backpropagation Algorithm:** Geoffrey Hinton and others publish work on backpropagation, enabling multi-layer neural networks to learn from data—an essential breakthrough for deep learning.

## The Second AI Winter (Late 1980s–1990s)

- **Expert systems** struggled with:
  - Inability to learn from new data.
  - Fragility—could make serious mistakes with unusual inputs.
- Shift from mainframes to PCs as expert systems became part of general business software.
- **Neural networks** faced scaling issues; backpropagation was difficult to implement for large datasets and networks.
- These challenges led to another decline in excitement and funding—the **second AI Winter**.

## Looking Ahead

- The next phase (late 1990s and 2000s) saw the rise of machine learning and, later, deep learning, which overcame many earlier limitations and led to today's AI boom.
- The history of AI is marked by cycles of optimism, setbacks, and renewed progress driven by new ideas and technologies.
