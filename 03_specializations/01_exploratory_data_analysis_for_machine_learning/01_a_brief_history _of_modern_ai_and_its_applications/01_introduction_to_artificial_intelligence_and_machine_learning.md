# Introduction to Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

## Key Concepts

- **Artificial Intelligence (AI):**  
  Any program or system that can sense, reason, act, and adapt. AI is the broadest field, encompassing any machine that exhibits intelligent behavior, not just those that learn from data.

- **Machine Learning (ML):**  
  A subset of AI. ML systems learn and improve from experience (data) rather than being explicitly programmed for every scenario.

- **Deep Learning (DL):**  
  A subset of ML. DL refers specifically to algorithms that use multi-layered neural networks to learn from large amounts of data.

  > **Hierarchy:**  
  > Deep Learning ⊆ Machine Learning ⊆ Artificial Intelligence

## Historical Context and Progress

- The journey to modern AI has involved cycles of excitement and disappointment ("AI winters"), but recent advances have led to today's AI boom.
- Two major breakthroughs:
  - **Image Processing:**  Since 2015, computers have surpassed humans in classifying images (e.g., distinguishing cats from dogs).
  - **Machine Translation:**  Modern AI can translate text between languages with near-human accuracy, handling complex word order and phrasing.

- These tasks were extremely challenging for classical ML models, which struggled with feature extraction (images) and language structure (translation).

## Why Now? — Drivers of the AI Boom

- **Deep learning** has overcome many limitations of classical ML.
- Advances in **data storage** and **computational power** have enabled training of large neural networks.
- AI is now impacting nearly every industry, similar to the transformative effect of electricity.

## Real-World Applications

- **Advertising:** Targeted marketing
- **Retail:** Supply chain optimization
- **Transportation:** Self-driving cars
- **Smart Homes:** Voice command, entertainment, security

## Definitions

- **AI (Merriam-Webster):**  
  "A branch of computer science dealing with the simulation of intelligent behavior in computers."
- **AI (Wikipedia):**  
  "Applied when a machine mimics cognitive functions that humans associate with other human minds, such as learning and problem solving."

- **Not all AI is ML:**  
  Example: Rule-based systems (do not learn from data).
