# History of Machine Learning and Deep Learning

## The Rise of Classical Machine Learning (1990s–2000s)

- In the 1990s and 2000s, AI solutions using classical machine learning techniques began to achieve real-world success.
- ML was successfully applied to:
  - Speech recognition
  - Medical diagnosis
  - Robotics
  - Many other areas
- AI algorithms became integrated into larger systems and found widespread industrial use.

## Key Milestones

- **1996:** IBM's Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov, marking a major milestone in AI's ability to outperform humans in complex games.
- **Google's PageRank Algorithm:** Revolutionized web search by ranking pages based on their importance in the network. (Recommended to look up PageRank for those interested in network algorithms.)

## The Deep Learning Revolution

- **2006:** Geoffrey Hinton and colleagues overcome previous deep learning limitations (like exploding and vanishing gradients) with innovations such as unsupervised pre-training.
  - Neural networks are rebranded as **deep learning** as it becomes possible to train much deeper (multi-layer) networks.
- **2009:** Introduction of the **ImageNet** database, providing millions of labeled images and enabling large-scale visual recognition research.
- **2010:** The first ImageNet competition is held, allowing algorithms to compete on challenging visual recognition tasks.

## The Climax: AlexNet and the Deep Learning Boom

- **2012:** The deep learning model **AlexNet** (using convolutional neural networks) wins the ImageNet competition with a top-5 error rate of 15.3%, more than 10 percentage points lower than the next best competitor.
  - This breakthrough marks the beginning of deep learning's dominance in AI.

## Rapid Progress and Applications

- **2013:** Deep learning models learn conceptual meanings of words from massive text datasets, advancing natural language understanding.
- **2014:** Breakthroughs in machine translation using recurrent neural networks (RNNs), achieving what was once a major goal (and cause of early AI winters).
  - Advances in web search, document summarization, and translation.
  - Stanford researchers develop models that can generate descriptive captions for images, moving beyond simple object recognition.
- **2015:** Google releases **TensorFlow**, a powerful and accessible deep learning library.
- **2016:** DeepMind's **AlphaGo** defeats a world champion Go player, a feat previously thought to be decades away due to the game's complexity.
- **2018:** Waymo (Google's self-driving car project) launches a taxi service in Phoenix, Arizona.
- **2019:** IBM's **Project Debater** competes with a human champion in a live debate, demonstrating AI's ability to understand, argue, and rebut complex topics.
  - TensorFlow 2.0 is released, integrating the high-level Keras API for even greater accessibility and flexibility.

## Summary

- The 1990s and 2000s saw the rise of classical machine learning and the integration of AI into industry.
- The 2010s marked the deep learning revolution, with breakthroughs in computer vision, natural language processing, and reinforcement learning.
- Modern AI is now achieving feats once thought impossible, with rapid progress and real-world impact across many domains.
